FAQ

Who can attend Trek?

Trek is planned for eligible youth in the Enoch Utah West Stake who will be 14 years of age or older

When is Trek?

Trek is planned for June 2027. Exact dates and departure/return times will be posted as soon as they are finalized

Where are we going?

We’ll be traveling to Church historic sites along the Mormon Trail in Wyoming. Our anticipated experience includes Martin’s Cove, Sixth Crossing, the Sweetwater River, Independence Rock, Rock Creek Hollow, and other historic locations as scheduling permits.

What will we do on Trek?

We’ll pull handcarts, visit historic sites, learn about the pioneers, participate in devotionals and spiritual experiences, eat and travel together as Trek families, serve one another, and have opportunities for personal reflection and fun.

Do I need to be in good physical condition?

Trek will involve considerable walking, pulling handcarts, uneven terrain, and changing weather conditions. You don’t need to be an athlete, but everyone should begin preparing physically now. Walking regularly and gradually increasing your distance is a great place to start.

What if I have a medical condition or physical limitation?

Please don’t assume that a medical condition automatically prevents you from participating. Complete the Participant Information Form and provide accurate information about medical conditions, medications, allergies, limitations, or accommodations you may need. Trek leadership and our medical team will use this information to help us plan appropriately.

What if I have food allergies or dietary restrictions?

Please identify all food allergies and dietary restrictions on the Participant Information Form, including their severity. This will help our food and medical teams prepare appropriately.

What is a Trek family?

Youth will be organized into smaller Trek families led by an adult Ma and Pa. Your Trek family will pull a handcart together, eat together, participate in activities together, and look out for one another throughout the experience.

Will we cook our own food as Trek families?

Our current plan is to prepare and distribute meals from a central food-service area. Trek families will receive their food and then eat together as families. This allows us to efficiently serve a large group while preserving the Trek family experience.

Will we have water while trekking?

Yes. Hydration will be a major priority. Each handcart will carry two 5-gallon insulated water coolers, and plans will be in place to refill water throughout Trek.

What should I bring?

A detailed packing list will be provided before Trek. Participants should expect to bring appropriate clothing, sturdy footwear, sleeping equipment, personal hygiene items, and other basic Trek necessities. Please don’t purchase special equipment yet unless instructed; more information is coming.

Do we have to wear pioneer clothing?

Specific clothing expectations will be provided as plans are finalized. Our goal will be clothing that contributes to the Trek experience while also being modest, practical, comfortable, and appropriate for Wyoming weather and trail conditions.

What should I wear while traveling to and from Wyoming?

Participants will not wear pioneer clothing while traveling to or from Trek. Youth and adult leaders should travel in comfortable, modest, weather-appropriate clothing suitable for the bus ride. Pioneer clothing will be packed with your Trek gear and changed into at the appropriate time after we arrive. At the conclusion of Trek, participants will have an opportunity to change back into clean, regular travel clothing before the trip home.

Can I bring my phone?

We encourage youth to leave personal electronics at home so they can step away from everyday distractions and fully participate in the Trek experience. Youth should not plan to use cell phones, earbuds/headphones, gaming devices, smartwatches, or other personal electronics during Trek. Necessary communication with parents will be handled through Trek leadership, and leaders will have communication equipment available for emergencies. Designated Trek photographers and leaders may use electronic devices as needed for safety, communication, and documenting the experience.

What does “Yoked with Him” mean?

Our Trek theme comes from Matthew 11:28–30, where Jesus Christ invites us to come unto Him, take His yoke upon us, and learn of Him. Trek will give us opportunities to pull together, carry burdens, serve others, and learn what it means to rely upon the Savior’s strength rather than trying to walk through life alone.

How should I prepare spiritually?

Begin now. Pray, study the scriptures, learn about your ancestors, and consider what being “Yoked with Him” means personally to you. We especially encourage participants to attend the temple as often as circumstances allow and find ways to connect with their ancestral heritage.

What if I’m not sure whether I can attend?

Please complete the Participant Information Form anyway. The form allows you to indicate whether you are planning to attend, probably attending, unsure, or not planning to attend. Early responses will help us estimate participation and plan Trek families, transportation, food, medical support, and other needs.

Where can I complete the Participant Information Form?

Youth and adults who may participate should each complete the Trek Participant Information Form individually. The form is linked below.
